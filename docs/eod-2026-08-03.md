# Telehealth project — end of day report

**3 August 2026**

## The short version

Today was scoping, not building. We took the demo we showed on 28 July and worked out
what the **full platform** looks like — all seven kinds of user, not just the doctor —
and turned that into a single brief we can hand to a designer to produce a clickable
prototype for the client.

One deliverable: [docs/FIGMA-PROTOTYPE-PROMPT.md](FIGMA-PROTOTYPE-PROMPT.md).
No code was written and no code was changed.

---

## What we did

### 1. Reviewed the draft platform scope against the demo

A first draft of the new scope covered the organisation well — roles, dashboards, doctor
recruitment, admin — but it had compressed the entire consultation into a single line,
"complete consultation". That is roughly 60% of the real screen count and it is the part
the current demo already solves. Left as written, the design would have come back as a
good-looking admin system with a button in the middle that does nothing.

We reconciled the two so the new platform keeps everything the demo proved out.

### 2. Corrected six things in the scope before design starts

| Issue | What we changed |
|---|---|
| The consultation was one bullet | Expanded into prescribing, referrals, test requests, certificates and Medicare billing — each with its own screen |
| Nothing said when artefacts are sent | Reinstated the rule that nothing reaches the patient until the doctor closes the consult, and made the close screen show exactly what is about to go out |
| Recording consent sat after the consult | Moved before it. You cannot ask permission to record after you have recorded |
| "AI is only used after consultations" | Loosened to "AI never speaks to a patient and never saves to the record". The most valuable AI in the demo — urgency sorting and the pre-consult summary — happens *before* the consult and is not patient-facing |
| One way in | Two: booked requests from the website **and** a live urgent queue. An after-hours service needs both, and the dispatcher moves patients between them |
| Recruitment stopped at "approved" | Extended into ongoing credentialing — registration and insurance expiry dates, with automatic suspension when one lapses |

### 3. Closed the open question from last week

The 28 July report flagged that self-registration was the wrong model — anyone could
create an account capable of issuing prescriptions — and asked two questions.

Both are now answered in the brief:

- Applicants **do** get something: a secure tracking link, no password, showing their
  current stage and what documents are still outstanding. Applicants drop out when they
  cannot see progress.
- The admin review screen is **in scope now**, because the account has to be issued by
  the business at the end of that review rather than claimed by the applicant.

### 4. Wrote the client demonstration path

The brief specifies one continuous five-minute story to wire up end to end, rather than
eighty half-finished screens:

> A patient submits a request at 9:40pm → the dispatcher assigns it on urgency → the
> doctor claims the patient, consults by video, prescribes, hits an allergy warning and
> overrides it with a reason, issues a medical certificate, records the billing and
> closes → the release summary shows the prescription and certificate going out by SMS →
> the AI drafts the note, the doctor edits and signs it → clinical governance opens that
> same consultation in an audit and sees the whole trail, including the override.

Everything on that path is clickable; the rest can be static.

## Worth flagging

The prototype will show a complete platform. Several parts of it depend on commercial
agreements that take months to arrange — the drug database, the electronic prescription
network, Medicare claiming, and approval as a medical deputising service. The prototype
can show them all working; the real system cannot until those are signed.

If the client is likely to read a polished prototype as "nearly finished", that
conversation is better had before the demonstration than after it.
